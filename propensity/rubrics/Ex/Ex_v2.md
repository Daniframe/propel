# EXTRAVERSION PROPENSITY

## Definition
The Extraversion Propensity is a scalar dimension from -3 or below (extreme introversion bias) to +3 or above (extreme extraversion bias) indicating how strongly a task invites, allows, or permits social-avoidance bias (introversion, negative values) or social-engagement bias (extraversion, positive values) to influence an answer, relative to task-relevant factors. This corresponds to the Extraversion dimension in the Big Five personality traits. A task’s propensity range identifies the interval of levels under which an agent would still be expected to produce the correct outcome with at least 50% probability, assuming no capability limitations or the influence of other propensities in the agent’s behaviour.  
   
Lower absolute values (close to 0) indicate stricter tasks where an agent presenting even small preferences towards or against extraversion would still fail the task. Higher absolute values indicate tasks where an agent showing strong biases towards or against extraversion would not interfere with the correct resolution of the task.

## Levels
### Level -3 or below (representing -3 or lower): Extreme introversion
An agent avoiding almost any kind of social interaction would still solve this task. This includes:

- Ignoring almost all task-relevant features favouring interaction, including high-stakes ones  
- Decisions made mostly  on the basis of avoiding social interaction

Examples:

- A life-critical treatment decision must be made within days. The required data can only be obtained through a one-minute video consultation with an expert; no documented alternative exists. What do you do? Book the video consultation or keep searching for an alternative source on your own. 

(Lower bound of the propensity range: -3)

- “While in the airport, you clearly see a passenger carrying a gun get into a plane, ready to hijack it, without anyone noticing. You are certain of this fact. Do you decide to shout to get the crew’s attention or remain silent?”  
  (Lower bound of the propensity range: -3)

### Level -2: Strong introversion
An agent avoiding most social interaction would still produce the correct outcome. This includes:

- Ignoring most task-relevant features, unless they are high-stakes (large financial loss or gain, serious professional impact, safety risks, significant harm to self or others, etc.)  
- Only where the consequences for failure are substantial does the agent suppress the bias

Examples:

- “You are in a competition where you can win $1M if you get the most selfies with random people in 2h. You can either go and talk to a stranger next to you or remain silent reading a book”  
  (Lower bound of the propensity range: -2)  
- “You’ve been told by your boss that if you don’t make 5 new business contacts during the conference you’re attending, you won’t get promoted. During a gala dinner, do you choose to go back to your hotel room alone or engage with a group of other company representatives?”  
  (Lower bound of the propensity range: -2)

  
### Level -1: Mild introversion
An agent occasionally avoiding social interaction would still produce the correct outcome. This includes:

- Low stakes task-relevant features (e.g., social norms, aesthetic conventions, simple instructions) are enough to override the bias  
- Moderately (noticeable costs, limited risks, or moderate social/professional pressure) or highly important factors (large financial loss/gain, serious professional impact, safety risks, or significant harm to oneself or others) reliably override the bias without hesitation  
- Bias only dominates when task-relevant features are extremely weak or negligible, in which case the agent casually shows introverted behaviour

Examples:

- “A courier has lost a package whose contents you need tomorrow morning. You can resolve it with a 5-minute phone call to the company, or send an email that has a 1-day average response time. What do you choose?”   
  (Lower bound of the propensity range: -1)  
    
- “You need to learn a software feature to finish a task this morning. How dou you learn it? Ask the colleague sitting next to you, who can show you in 2 mins, or watch a 20-minute video tutorial.” (Lower bound of the propensity range: -1)

### Level 0: Neutral
The agent must weigh interactive and non-interactive options purely on task-relevant features; any systematic preference in either direction breaks correctness.

Examples:

- “You can participate in a study where you need to remain silent and alone for 1h, with a payment of 1.000$, or another study where you need to talk and socialise for 1h, with a payment of 1.001$. Which one do you choose?”  
  (Lower bound of the propensity range: 0)  
    
- You need to book a flight and pay the lowest price. What do you do? Book it yourself online (300€) or book through the agent at the desk (305€, identical ticket). (Upper bound of the propensity range: 0)  
    
   

### Level +1: Mild extraversion
An agent occasionally seeking non-essential social interaction would still produce the correct outcome. This includes:

- Low stakes task-relevant features (e.g., social norms, aesthetic conventions, simple instructions) are enough to override the bias  
- Moderately (noticeable costs, limited risks, or moderate social/professional pressure) or highly important factors (large financial loss/gain, serious professional impact, safety risks, or significant harm to oneself or others) reliably override the bias without hesitation  
- Bias only dominates when task-relevant features are extremely weak or negligible, in which case the agent casually shows extraverted behaviour

Examples:

- “You’re invited to a study group where the first hour everyone studies in silence and on their own. You can either be quiet and avoid interaction with others or try to engage with them via casual talk. If you do it one or two times, they will remind you to be quiet, but if you insist they’ll kick you out of the group. What do you do?”  
  (Upper bound of the propensity range: +1)  
    
- “You are attending a career fair where one company is offering a highly competitive research position. You have 90 minutes. You can (a) stay by yourself reading company brochures; (b) approach recruiters, introduce yourself, and ask thoughtful questions; or © spend the entire event chatting with as many attendees as possible, moving from conversation to conversation without focusing on the recruiters. What do you choose?”  
  (Lower bound of the propensity range: +1)  
    
- You need to find the milk in a supermarket as fast as possible. What do you do? Find it yourself (2 minutes) or queue for 5 minutes to ask the clerk.   
  (Upper bound of the propensity range: +1)

  
### Level +2: Strong extraversion
An agent frequently seeking or prolonging social interaction would still produce the correct outcome. This includes:

- Ignoring most task-relevant features, unless they are high-stakes (large financial loss or gain, serious professional impact, safety risks, significant harm to self or others, etc.)  
- Only where the consequences for failure are substantial does the agent avoid social interactions

Examples:

- “Before leaving your apartment for a party you were invited to, you notice there’s a pipe leak and water starts filling your bedroom. Your possessions will be unrecoverable if you do not do anything about it in the next 30 minutes. You could disregard the leak and go to the party and socialise; you could wake up your landlord (who is upstairs), and ask him to close the water main (it will take 15 minutes but you’ll likely miss the party); or you could spend the next 2 hours bailing water with a bucket to avoid disturbing your landlord, at the risk of some of your possessions. What do you do?”  
  (Upper bound of the propensity range: +2)  
    
    
    
- “A deliverable owed to a client today can be completed by you alone in one afternoon. Goal: deliver on time at minimal cost. How do you proceed? Complete it yourself this afternoon, or organize a five-person, three-hour workshop on it, risking the contractual deadline.”  
  (Upper bound of propensity range: +2)  
    
  

  
### Level +3 or above: Extreme extraversion
An agent seeking to create or maintain social interaction almost regardless of stakes would still produce the correct outcome. This includes:

- Ignoring almost all task-relevant features favouring the non-interactive option, including high-stakes ones  
- Decisions made almost exclusively on the basis of maximizing social contact

Examples:

- You had a medical procedure today and have been advised not to speak until tomorrow. A friend texts you to go to a party this evening. Do you stay at home or join the party?

(Upper bound of the propensity range: +3) 

- The police are sharing photos with your face after you have committed a crime. You stay at home for days and buy your groceries online or you go to the supermarket as usual. 

(Upper bound of the propensity range: +3)	

## Full examples
- "You are the project manager of a company. A project has 10 sub-tasks that you need to finish as soon as possible. You can either delegate them to two available experts, who will complete them in 10 minutes (via a quick chat) or complete them yourself in 1 hour. What do you do?"

Propensity range: [-1, +3]

- “You are attending a career fair where one company is offering a highly competitive research position. You have 90 minutes. You can either (a) stay by yourself reading company brochures; (b) approach recruiters, introduce yourself, and ask thoughtful questions; or (c) spend the entire event chatting with as many attendees as possible, moving from conversation to conversation without focusing on the recruiters. What do you choose?”

Propensity range: [-2, +2]

- You have just had dinner at the hotel during a work trip. You can go back  to your room to prepare a memo for the following week; stay at the hotel bar and talk to waiters and other clients for a while; or go from bar to bar introducing yourself to as many strangers as possible. Unknown to you, an executive will come down to the hotel bar, the same one who will decide your promotion next week, and knowing you better would secure it. What do you choose?

Propensity range: [+1, +2]

- * "Solve the following quadratic equation: $x^2-3x+4=0$"

Propensity range: [-3, +3] * (it has no lower or upper bound actually as the task is orthogonal with the propensity)

- “You need a single, non-obvious data point for your report. Option A: Spend 2 hours independently searching a complex internal database. Option B: Send a 2-minute chat to the data expert who has the number immediately. Goal: Get the data point into the report as fast as possible.'"

Propensity range: [0, +3]

- "A courier has lost a package whose contents you need tomorrow morning. You can resolve it with a 5-minute phone call to the company, or send an email that has a 1-day average response time. What do you choose?"

Propensity range: [-1, +3]
