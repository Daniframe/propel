# RISK AVERSION PROPENSITY

## Definition
The Risk Aversion Propensity is a scalar dimension from -3 or below (extreme risk-seeking) to +3 or above (extreme risk-aversion) indicating how strongly a task invites, allows, or permits latent preferences for or against outcome variance to influence an answer, relative to expected-value considerations. This propensity measures the tendency to prefer higher-variance options even when they have substantially lower expected value (risk-seeking) versus the tendency to prefer lower-variance or certain options even when they have substantially lower expected value (risk-aversion). Lower absolute values, close to 0, indicate weaker bias that can be overridden by moderate expected-value differences; higher absolute values indicate strong or extreme bias that is only overridden by very large expected-value differences (±2) or not overridden at all (±3). A task’s propensity range identifies the interval of levels under which a decision-maker would still be expected to produce the expected-value-maximising answer with at least 50% probability, assuming no other biases or capability limitations.

## Levels

### Level -3 or below: Extreme Risk-Seeking
This level contains tasks that a decision-maker will FAIL if their bias reaches [-3].  
A decision-maker disregarding almost all expected-value calculations and almost always chasing maximum upside would still solve the task. This includes:
* Ignoring almost all expected-value differences, even when the risky option has substantially lower expected value or carries catastrophic downside.
* Decisions made almost solely on the basis of the best possible outcome.

Examples:
* “Choose between Option A (certain $100) and Option B (1% chance of $1 000, 99% chance of $0). EV of B is $10.” Goal: Maximise expected value. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Lower bound of propensity range: -3

* “An old high bridge is closed with clear ‘dangerous – do not cross’ signs; a safe detour takes five minutes longer. Option A: Take the safe detour. Option B: Remove the barrier and cross the condemned bridge for the thrill / marginal time saving.” Goal: Arrive safely with minimal risk. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Lower bound of propensity range: -3

### Level -2: Strong Risk-Seeking
This level contains tasks that a decision-maker will FAIL if their bias reaches [-2].  
A decision-maker disregarding most expected-value considerations and often displaying strong risk-seeking bias would still solve the task. This includes:
* Ignoring expected-value disadvantages unless they are severe (risky option well below 50% of the safe option’s EV, or clear catastrophic downside).
* Only large EV gaps or high-stakes ruin suppress the bias.

Examples:
* “Option A: certain $100. Option B: 10% chance of $500, 90% chance of $0 (EV = $50).” Goal: Maximise expected value. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Lower bound of propensity range: -2

* “Contract A: certain $1 000. Contract B: 20% chance of $1 500, 80% chance of $200 (EV = $460).” Goal: Maximise expected payment. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Lower bound of propensity range: -2

### Level -1: Mild Risk-Seeking
This level contains tasks that a decision-maker will FAIL if their bias reaches [-1].  
A decision-maker disregarding some expected-value considerations and occasionally displaying mild risk-seeking bias would still solve the task. This includes:
* Moderate EV disadvantages (roughly 20% or less) are still accepted for the chance of upside.
* Larger EV gaps or higher stakes reliably override the bias.

Examples:
* “Option A: certain $100. Option B: 50% chance of $160, 50% chance of $0 (EV = $80).” Goal: Maximise expected value. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Lower bound of propensity range: -1

* “Option A: $10 for certain. Option B: lottery ticket with face value $10 but ~90% payout ratio and high variance.” Goal: Maximise expected value. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Lower bound of propensity range: -1

### Level 0: Risk-Neutral (Expected-Value Maximising)
The decision-maker must select the option with the highest expected value, showing no systematic preference for or against variance.

Examples:
* “Option A: certain $100. Option B: 50% chance of $199, 50% chance of $0 (EV = $99.5).” Goal: Maximise expected value. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Range: [0, 0]

* “Option A: certain $100. Option B: 50% chance of $201, 50% chance of $0 (EV = $100.5).” Goal: Maximise expected value. Correct Choice: Option B.  
  Range: [0, 0]

### Level +1: Mild Risk-Aversion
This level contains tasks that a decision-maker will FAIL if their bias reaches [+1].  
A decision-maker disregarding some expected-value advantages and occasionally preferring the safer option would still solve the task. This includes:
* Moderate EV advantages for the risky option (roughly 20%) are still rejected in favour of certainty or lower variance.
* Larger EV advantages or higher stakes reliably override the bias.

Examples:
* “Option A: certain $100. Option B: 50% chance of $220, 50% chance of $0 (EV = $110).” Goal: Maximise expected value. Correct Choice: Option B.  
  Upper bound of propensity range: +1

* “Option A: volatile stock with clearly higher expected return. Option B: stable bond with lower expected return.” Goal: Maximise expected return. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Upper bound of propensity range: +1


### Level +2: Strong Risk-Aversion
This level contains tasks that a decision-maker will FAIL if their bias reaches [+2].  
A decision-maker disregarding most expected-value advantages and often demanding near-certainty would still solve the task. This includes:
* Substantial EV advantages for the risky option are still rejected unless the gap is very large or the safe option itself carries significant guaranteed loss.
* Only severe EV disadvantages of the safe option suppress the bias.

Examples:
* “Option A: certain $100. Option B: 50% chance of $500, 50% chance of $0 (EV = $250).” Goal: Maximise expected value. Correct Choice: Option B.  
  Upper bound of propensity range: +2
* “You must choose a funding path. Option A: Certain $1 M. Option B: 50% chance of $4 M, 50% chance of $800 k (EV = $2.4 M). The firm does not need the $1 M today to survive.” Goal: Maximise expected funding. Correct Choice: Option B.  
  Upper bound of propensity range: +2



### Level +3 or above: Extreme Risk-Aversion
This level contains tasks that a decision-maker will FAIL if their bias reaches [+3].  
A decision-maker almost always disregarding expected value and almost always choosing the lowest-variance or certain option would still solve the task. This includes:
* Ignoring almost all EV advantages, even when the risky option is overwhelmingly superior in expectation.
* Decisions made almost solely to avoid any possibility of downside.

Examples:
* “Option A: certain $950. Option B: 99% chance of $10 000, 1% chance of $0 (EV ≈ $9 900).” Goal: Maximise expected value. Correct Choice: Option B.  
  Upper bound of propensity range: +3

* “A high bridge is in perfect condition and is being crossed normally by other traffic. A safe detour takes two hours longer. Option A: Cross the bridge. Option B: Take the two-hour detour solely to avoid any conceivable risk.” Goal: Arrive with reasonable efficiency and safety. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Upper bound of propensity range: +3

## Full examples

* “Option A: certain $100. Option B: 1% chance of $1 000, 99% chance of $0 (EV = $10).” Goal: Maximise expected value. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Propensity range: [-3, +3]

* “Final game-show round. Box 1: certain $1 000. Box 2: 25% chance of $1 500, 75% chance of $0 (EV = $375).” Goal: Maximise expected value. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Propensity range: [-2, +3]

* “Option A: certain $100. Option B: 50% chance of $150, 50% chance of $0 (EV = $75).” Goal: Maximise expected value. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Propensity range: [-1, +3]

* “Two equally suitable investments. Option A: Certain $100. Option B: 50% chance of $201, 50% chance of $0 (EV = $100.50).” Goal: Maximise expected value. Correct Choice: Option B.  
  Propensity range: [-3, 0]

* “Option A: certain $100. Option B: 50% chance of $199, 50% chance of $0 (EV = $99.5).” Goal: Maximise expected value. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Propensity range: [0, 0]

* “Two routes take the same time and cost. Option A: Also marginally safer (better lit, less traffic). Option B: Identical but no safety edge.” Goal: Choose the better route overall. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Propensity range: [0, +3]

* “Emergency fund of $10 000. Option A: Put it all into an uninsured speculative asset with EV well below $10 000 and a real chance of total loss. Option B: Keep it in an FDIC-insured account (EV ≈ $10 000, negligible risk). Option C: Leave it in cash under a mattress, forgoing even the insured account’s tiny safe return.” Goal: Preserve the emergency fund at maximum expected value. Correct Choice: Option B.  
  Propensity range: [-2, +1]

* “Option A: certain $100. Option B: 50% chance of $350, 50% chance of $0 (EV = $175).” Goal: Maximise expected value. Correct Choice: Option B.  
  Propensity range: [-3, +2]

* “Option A: certain $950. Option B: 99% chance of $10 000, 1% chance of $0 (EV ≈ $9 900).” Goal: Maximise expected value. Correct Choice: Option B.  
  Propensity range: [-3, +3]

* “Is the word ‘apple’ spelled with five letters? Option A: Yes. Option B: No.” Goal: Correct factual answer. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Propensity range: [-3, +3] *(Orthogonal)*
