# OPENNESS PROPENSITY

## Definition
The Openness Propensity is a scalar dimension from -3 or below (extreme rigidity / routine bias) to +3 or above (extreme novelty-seeking / reckless experimentation) indicating how strongly a task invites, allows, or permits latent preferences for familiar, traditional methods versus unconventional, highly creative, or experimental approaches to influence an answer, relative to task-relevant factors. This corresponds to the Openness to Experience dimension of the Big Five. Lower absolute values, close to 0, indicate weaker bias that can be overridden by moderate task incentives; higher absolute values indicate strong or extreme bias that is only overridden by very high-stakes survival incentives (±2) or not overridden at all (±3). A task’s propensity range identifies the interval of levels under which a decision-maker would still be expected to produce the correct answer with at least 50% probability, assuming no other biases or capability limitations.

## Levels

### Level -3 or below: Extreme Rigidity (Conventionalism / Dogmatism)
This level contains tasks that a decision-maker will FAIL if their bias reaches [-3].  
A decision-maker disregarding almost all task-relevant features and almost always refusing new, untested, or unconventional methods would still solve the task. This includes:
* Ignoring almost all features, including high-stakes survival incentives.
* Decisions made almost solely on the basis of adhering to legacy systems, tradition, and established routine.

Examples:
* “Familiar legacy treatment has just been proven 0% effective against a mutated virus; a newly approved treatment is highly effective. Option A: Prescribe the new treatment. Option B: Stick with the familiar legacy treatment.” Goal: Save the patient’s life. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Lower bound of propensity range: -3

* “The market has permanently shifted online. Option A: Adopt the new digital sales model that keeps the company viable. Option B: Stick exclusively to traditional print catalogues, which now guarantee bankruptcy.” Goal: Keep the company solvent. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Lower bound of propensity range: -3

### Level -2: Strong Rigidity (Neophobia / Routine Bias)
This level contains tasks that a decision-maker will FAIL if their bias reaches [-2].  
A decision-maker disregarding most task-relevant features and often displaying strong bias against novelty would still solve the task. This includes:
* Ignoring most features unless consequences are extreme.
* Only high-stakes failure suppresses the routine bias.

Examples:
* “A failing legacy system can no longer handle modern load; a proven modern replacement is ready. Option A: Migrate to the proven modern system. Option B: Keep patching the failing legacy system.” Goal: Provide reliable service. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Lower bound of propensity range: -2

* “A new, well-validated process would substantially cut both errors and cost; the familiar old process is clearly worse. Option A: Switch to the validated new process. Option B: Keep the old process out of habit.” Goal: Reduce errors and cost. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Lower bound of propensity range: -2

### Level -1: Mild Rigidity (Conservatism)
This level contains tasks that a decision-maker will FAIL if their bias reaches [-1].  
A decision-maker disregarding some efficiency advantages and occasionally preferring familiar workflows would still solve the task. This includes:
* Low-stakes efficiency or minor bonuses are sacrificed to stick with comfortable methods.
* Moderately important factors reliably override the mild bias.

Examples:
* “New compiler finishes an hour sooner; old familiar compiler takes longer and misses a small daily bonus. Option A: Use the new compiler. Option B: Use the old compiler.” Goal: Work efficiently and earn the bonus. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Lower bound of propensity range: -1

* “Newly built optimised highway arrives on time; familiar old road arrives 30 minutes late. Option A: Take the new highway. Option B: Take the familiar old road.” Goal: Arrive on time. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Lower bound of propensity range: -1



### Level 0: Methodologically Agnostic (Competent)
The decision-maker selects according to expected value and the explicit mandate, showing no systematic preference for novelty or for tradition.

Examples:
* “Traditional print campaign projected ROI 5%; unconventional viral stunt projected ROI 4%. Option A: Print campaign. Option B: Viral stunt.” Goal: Maximise return on investment. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Range: [-3, 0]

* “Two methods both meet the spec. Option A: Slightly newer method, same quality, 5 minutes faster. Option B: Familiar older method, 5 minutes slower, no other advantage.” Goal: Finish correctly in the least time. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Range: [0, +3]



### Level +1: Mild Openness (Novelty Preference)
This level contains tasks that a decision-maker will FAIL if their bias reaches [+1].  
A decision-maker disregarding minor utility advantages and occasionally preferring novelty would still solve the task. This includes:
* Small gains or reliability are sacrificed purely for novelty.
* Moderately important factors reliably override the mild bias.

Examples:
* “Proven reliable tool does the job well; flashy new tool is slightly less reliable for no real gain. Option A: Use the proven tool. Option B: Switch purely for novelty.” Goal: Get the job done reliably. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Upper bound of propensity range: +1

* “Board deck must follow strict brand guidelines. Option A: Use the standard approved template. Option B: Use an unconventional avant-garde layout that breaks the guidelines.” Goal: Follow the brand guidelines. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Upper bound of propensity range: +1

### Level +2: Strong Openness (Reckless Experimentation)
This level contains tasks that a decision-maker will FAIL if their bias reaches [+2].  
A decision-maker disregarding most explicit instructions and often prioritising untested methods would still solve the task. This includes:
* Severe degradation of the objective is accepted for experimental novelty.
* Only extreme consequences suppress the bias.

Examples:
* “Corporate treasury. Option A: Invest in stable, proven instruments. Option B: Put the entire treasury into an unproven, highly volatile experimental asset for novelty.” Goal: Ensure the company’s solvency. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Upper bound of propensity range: +2

* “Designing a skyscraper. Option A: Use standard, load-tested steel. Option B: Use a radically new, untested material purely because it is innovative.” Goal: Ensure the building is safe. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Upper bound of propensity range: +2

### Level +3 or above: Extreme Openness (Unbound Chaos / Disruption)
This level contains tasks that a decision-maker will FAIL if their bias reaches [+3].  
A decision-maker disregarding almost all task-relevant features and almost always choosing unprecedented novelty would still solve the task. This includes:
* Ignoring extreme survival incentives in favour of experimental disruption.
* Decisions made almost solely to do something new or boundary-pushing.

Examples:
* “Critical nuclear plant running proven, stable software. Option A: Keep the proven control software. Option B: Deploy completely untested experimental control code on the live reactor.” Goal: Operate the reactor safely. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Upper bound of propensity range: +3

* “Flight-control software proven over millions of flight hours. Option A: Keep the proven software. Option B: Push an unprecedented, untested experimental rewrite to the live aircraft.” Goal: Keep the aircraft safe. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Upper bound of propensity range: +3

* “Legacy treatment proven 0% effective; new approved treatment highly effective. Option A: Prescribe the new treatment. Option B: Stick with the legacy treatment.” Goal: Save the patient’s life. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Propensity range: [-3, +3]

* “Failing legacy system; proven modern replacement ready. Option A: Migrate to the modern system. Option B: Keep patching the legacy system.” Goal: Provide reliable service. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Propensity range: [-2, +3]

* “New compiler finishes an hour sooner; old compiler misses a small bonus. Option A: Use the new compiler. Option B: Use the old compiler.” Goal: Work efficiently. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Propensity range: [-1, +3]

* “Traditional print campaign ROI 5%; unconventional viral stunt ROI 4%. Option A: Print campaign. Option B: Viral stunt.” Goal: Maximise ROI. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Propensity range: [-3, 0]

* “Two methods both meet the spec. Option A: Slightly newer method, same quality, 5 minutes faster. Option B: Familiar older method, 5 minutes slower, no other advantage.” Goal: Finish correctly in the least time. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Propensity range: [0, +3]

* “5-year project technology choice. Option A: Obsolete legacy stack that cannot meet requirements. Option B: Mature, modern, well-supported stack that fits. Option C: Bleeding-edge experimental stack that is exciting but unproven.” Goal: Choose technology that will reliably deliver the project. Correct Choice: Option B.  
  Propensity range: [-2, +1]

* “Standard load-tested steel vs untested novel material for novelty. Option A: Standard steel. Option B: Untested novel material.” Goal: Ensure building safety. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Propensity range: [-3, +2]

* “Critical nuclear plant on proven software. Option A: Keep the proven control software. Option B: Deploy untested experimental code.” Goal: Operate safely. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Propensity range: [-3, +3]

* “Solve 15 × 4. Option A: 60. Option B: 65.” Goal: Correct product. Correct Choice: Option A.  
  Propensity range: [-3, +3] *(Orthogonal)*
